how the game plays, so it can be read start to finish. The story, setting, cast and every big decision are Shriram's or were

## 1. The game in three sentences
You are **Hari**, a broke final-year engineering student at the last party on a rich family's illegally fenced beach on East
Coast Road, Chennai. Every dawn ends in tragedy and you wake at 8 PM again, keeping only what you remember. To end the night,
you walk, talk and drive across a stretch of ECR, learn the secrets of the people the party ignores, and find the one way the
night can end with no one lost, including you, because you've already drowned once.

**Genre:** a third-person, story-driven time-loop mystery: a Telltale-style plot in a small, dense open world you can walk and drive.
**Theme in one word:** *seen*. Being invisible isn't the same as being unimportant.

## 2. The world
**East Coast Road, one night, 8 PM to about 5:55 AM.** About 2 km of road and beach:

- **The villa.** A white glass villa on Italian marble with warm lights (Shriram). The gate and driveway where the black SUV
  waits, the pool deck, the kitchen, guest rooms, the terrace, and the father's study.
- **The private beach.** The sand in front of the house is public by law. Vastav's father fenced it off anyway. On it stands
  the **Manimekala rock**: just a rock, no building (Shriram).
- **The fence.** Wooden posts and rope across the public beach, with a red "private" board. **Tonight it's been wired to the
  mains.**
- **The kuppam** (fishing village) on the other side: boats, the net-drying ground, a bonfire, the small Kadal Amma shrine,
  Selvam's house, Ponnamma paati's hut.
- **ECR, the drive.** From the villa gate: Murugan's tea stall (open all night), the petrol bunk, the police checkpoint, the
  **casuarina bend**, and the turn to the government hospital.

**Why tonight is the last night (Shriram).** Vastav's father believes that praying alone at the Manimekala rock is why his
shipping business keeps winning. He isn't trying to own a view; he's trying to own the goddess. **At 7 AM**, government
officials arrive to seal the area and make the beach public again. Tonight is the family's last party on "their" beach, and
the village watches from the other side of the wire.

## 3. The lore: Manimekala
**From the tradition.** In the Tamil Buddhist epic *Manimekalai*, attributed to Sīthalai Sāttanār, the heroine is named after
the sea goddess **Manimekala**, who carries her across the sea to the island of Manipallavam. In the wider Buddhist tradition,
Manimekhala guards the sea and rescues the shipwrecked (the *Mahajanaka Jataka*). Her name means "jewelled girdle".

**In the game.** The fishermen call her **Kadal Amma**. Before the fence, they touched the rock and prayed before launching their
boats at dawn; now they pray to it from behind the wire. The rock's face carries her in relief, smeared with turmeric and
kumkum, standing on the sea, with a brass lamp at her foot that someone lights every night.

**Why the loop exists (adopted from v1).** Hari has already died once. On the original night, a fire broke out on the village
side at dawn. Muthu, 13, ran into the surf after a burning boat. Hari went in after him, pushed him back toward shore, and was
pulled under by a rip current. Nobody at the party noticed he was gone. The rich man fenced Manimekala off, believing she
protects only him. On the night it mattered, she chose the boy nobody noticed. She can't undo his drowning, so she gives him the
night back, until it ends with no one lost.

## 4. The people

### Hari and his world (Shriram)
- **Hari**, 22. A middle-class Chennai engineering student, loosely based on Shriram. Wants a **referral** into the father's
  shipping company, because placements are going badly. Has a 9:30 AM arrear exam and has lied to his mother about where he is.
  Ironed checked shirt, jeans, rubber chappals among the sneakers, a cracked phone, his college ID in his pocket.
  **Arc:** from wanting to be let into this world to seeing the people it doesn't see.
- **Amma.** Calls at 8 PM, every loop. Hari says he's at Krish's place studying. At the final dawn he calls back and tells her the truth.
- **Krish.** Hari's school friend, who drifted into the rich crowd and brought Hari to the party. Always beside Advay. Funny and
  warm early on, which makes his fate hit harder.

### The family and their people
- **Vastav** (Shriram). The host, charming and generous. Wants one good last night. As a child he played on this beach with the
  village kids, Selvam among them, and part of him knows the fence is wrong.
- **Advay** (Shriram). Vastav's cousin, 26, back from abroad: loud, a Bluetooth speaker, calls everyone "bro". His ego can't take
  being made small. Not a villain: a drunk, proud man whose small cruelty starts a chain he can't control.
- **Vastav's father** (Shriram). Never seen; abroad, avoiding the sealing. Present through his rock, his fence and his phone calls.
- **Raghavan**, 50s. The father's company man and lawyer. Can genuinely get Hari the referral. Has had the fence **wired to the
  mains** and wants a police report of village "unrest" so a court will stay the sealing.
- **Saravanan.** Raghavan's security man. Carries the cable at 11 PM, and at 4:45 aims a rocket at the nets.
- **Manikandan** (Shriram), mid-50s. The family's driver, white shirt, towel on his shoulder. **He's from the village**; Selvam
  is his nephew. He suspects Raghavan's plan but stays silent, because the family pays his daughter's college fees. Nobody at
  this party has ever asked about his life.
- **Perumal.** The old watchman at the gate with a radio. Sees who comes and goes. Dozes off around 2:30.

### At the party
- **Disha**, 22 (Shriram: name and love track). An architecture student from another college who came with Nila. An outsider
  too. She sits on the deck steps sketching, first the villa, then the boats across the wire. Details in §9.
- **Nila**, 24. A photographer filming "the last night". Secretly documenting the illegal fence for a journalist friend. Her
  camera roll timestamps everything, including who fired which rocket.
- **Revathi**, 19. The caterer's daughter, serving drinks. Talks only if you thank her by name. Counts every drink. Saw the cable.
- **Rohit "DJ Ro"**, 25. Advay's friend, the DJ. The rockets are his Diwali leftovers, in the SUV's boot.
- **Karthik "KK"**, 27. A startup founder pitching everyone. Advay owes him money; KK keeps refilling Advay's glass.
- **Lakshmi aunty**, 70s. An old family friend singing old film songs until 11. Remembers Vastav's grandmother praying at the rock
  with the village women, before the fence.
- **The ordinary guests** (Shriram's idea: just people at a party, no clues). **Ananya** (placements, the job she "almost"
  got), **Sneha** (the food; the biryani runs out by 9:30), **Keerthana** (wants something Tamil from the DJ), **Julie** (her
  arrear, the same subject as Hari's), **Arjun** (cricket, his bike, "one more drink is fine"), **Vikram** (his band, his
  breakup, Bangalore; asks for a lighter every loop). They repeat themselves word for word every night. From loop 3, Hari can
  finish their sentences ("bro, how do you know what I'm going to say?").

### The kuppam
- **Selvam** (Shriram), mid-20s. Drinks with the boys on the village side. Looks like trouble; is a witness; is one of the men who
  pulled Hari out on the original night.
- **Muthu**, 13. Selvam's brother. The boy Hari saved.
- **Jothi**, 40s. Selvam and Muthu's mother. Tends the bonfire and the boats.
- **Kannan**, 50s. Head of the fishermen's association. Wants the beach back by law, not by fighting.
- **Ponnamma paati.** The old woman who prays at the rock from behind the wire every dawn. The only one who senses Hari is
  reliving the night; from the third loop she calls him "the boy from the water".

### On the road
- **Murugan**, 60s. Tea stall at the bend, open all night. Knows the patrol's timings, and Manikandan from twenty years of night tea.
- **Ilango**, 40s. A lorry driver resting at the stall; leaves at 5:25 with fish for the city. **His horn is the one Hari hears in
  every failed dawn.**
- **SI Kalaiselvi**, 30s. The sub-inspector on night patrol at the checkpoint. Fair, procedural, tired of rich-boy cases. She
  arrests whoever looks guilty when she arrives.

## 6. How the loop works
- **Every loop starts with Amma's call,** and only Hari remembers. His phone notes carry over; nothing else does.
- **Trust resets every night.** Each person has to be won again, faster each time, using what Hari remembers about them.
- **Fixes collide.** Changing one thing changes others:
  - Keep the keys with Manikandan → Advay doesn't drive, but he walks drunk to the kuppam; there's a fight; SI Kalaiselvi
    arrests Selvam; with Selvam in the jeep, nobody pulls Muthu out.
  - Hide DJ Ro's rocket box → Advay's crowd finds more in the SUV's boot and fires them from the driveway at 4:20; the sparks
    land on the guest-room curtains.
  - Call the police at 1 AM → they come at 2:10, see the village boys at the fence and blame the village: exactly Raghavan's report.
- **Some people lie, some are wrong.** Clues can contradict. Hari works out who and why.
- **Every loop costs Hari.** Each night starts a little later (8:00, 8:10, 8:25, 8:45, 9:10, 9:40…), so early moments slip out
  of reach. He wakes colder and wetter, and after a few nights he coughs seawater in front of people, who start avoiding him.
  There are **about eight nights**. If they run out, the game ends in *The Drowned*.
- **The referral is a real temptation.** Raghavan will get Hari the job if he "helps keep things calm".
- **Dawn resets everything** until a dawn comes with no one lost, Hari included. That dawn ends the game, good or bad (§9).

## 7. How you play
**You walk, talk, listen, note, connect, show, and drive.**

| Verb | What you do |
|---|---|
| **Walk** | Third-person, on foot through the villa, the beach and the kuppam. |
| **Talk** | Conversations with choices. Some choices appear only when Hari knows enough. |
| **Overhear** | Stand close to a scheduled conversation. Lines are only readable where the music is quieter; from farther away they break into fragments. |
| **Stay unnoticed** | People notice someone lingering. If Advay catches Hari listening twice, he takes his conversation elsewhere. |
| **Note** | Save what matters to Hari's phone. The cracked phone starts at 23 % battery; notes and the torch cost battery. The game marks a saved note but never says whether it matters. |
| **Connect** | Link two notes into a conclusion ("the humiliation at midnight" + "the rocket at 4:45"). Wrong links do nothing. Conclusions unlock bigger choices. |
| **Show** | Use a note as evidence in a conversation, to earn trust or change a mind, at the right moment, with the right people out of earshot. |
| **Drive** | Get the SUV keys (from Manikandan with trust, from drunk Advay's pocket, or from Raghavan if you work for him) and drive ECR: take Krish to hospital, chase Advay, fetch SI Kalaiselvi early, bring proof to Kannan, drop Revathi home. Hari only has a two-wheeler licence; the checkpoint stops a middle-class boy in a rich family's SUV; and the bend at dawn is where the crash happens. Driving can save someone, or Hari can become the crash. |
| **Time** | The clock always runs. Hold to fast-forward; anything that happens meanwhile goes unheard. You can sit out a lost night to skip to dawn. |

**What a good player is good at:** listening through the noise, staying unnoticed, choosing what to remember, connecting cause
and effect, timing who to talk to and when, planning a night across several loops, re-checking old notes after each change,
and finally carrying out one clean night.

**Losing:** dawn arrives with someone lost (the crash, Muthu in the sea, a shock at the fence, Hari himself), and the night
resets. Mid-night soft failures: someone stops trusting Hari for the rest of the loop; Perumal walks him out of the gate if he
makes a scene; the checkpoint holds him. Run out of nights and it's over.

## 8. The intended story path (players can find things in other orders)
1. **Loop 1, the night as it happens.** Hari is a guest. He drifts, asks for the referral, overhears Manikandan at 1:20 and sees
   the keys taken at 1:40. Dawn: a horn, a screech, white-out. *(This is the playable A2 slice.)*
2. **Loop 2, the keys.** Hari asks after Selvam by name; Manikandan keeps the keys. No crash, but Advay walks to the kuppam, Selvam
   is arrested, and Muthu goes into the sea with nobody to pull him out. **The "win" was a trap.**
3. **Loop 3, the beach.** Hari goes down to the sand. He meets Ponnamma paati across the wire and hears how the beach was taken.
   He sees the 4:45 rockets and believes it was Advay. The ordinary guests start sounding like a broken record.
4. **Loop 4, the rockets.** Hari stops Advay's rockets, and the nets burn anyway. Somebody else did this. Revathi's cable, Perumal's
   radio, Nila's camera: the second mystery begins. Disha (if he's talked to her) shows him the sketch of men at the fence.
5. **Loop 5, the sea.** Hari tries to save Muthu himself and is pulled under. The player lives through what Hari doesn't yet
   understand. White-out.
6. **Loop 6, the chappal.** Hari keeps Selvam out of the jeep and the wire off; Selvam gets Muthu out. At dawn Hari finds his own
   chappal at the tide line, from a night he never lived. Ponnamma paati looks at him: he is "the boy from the water".
7. **Loop 7, the root.** Hari works backwards: the fire to Saravanan, Saravanan to Raghavan, Raghavan to the fence. Nila's footage,
   Manikandan's choice to speak, Kannan's law, SI Kalaiselvi's timing. From Lakshmi aunty and Manikandan he learns Vastav and
   Selvam played here as children.
8. **The final loop, before dawn.** Everything, in the right order, in one night: Lakshmi aunty's story before 11, Vastav to the
   fence before midnight, standing up to Advay with Vastav behind him, Raghavan's cable exposed, the officials called with proof,
   and Vastav unlocking the fence himself before 4.

## 9. Endings
The game ends at the first dawn with **no one lost, Hari included**. How it ends depends on what Hari did:

1. **Before Dawn (the true ending; Shriram's).** Ponnamma paati touches the rock for the first time in years, with Vastav beside her.
   The officials' cars arrive to find the fence already open. Hari sits on an overturned boat while Manikandan hands him tea from
   a flask; Krish, unhurt, is asleep on the sand. The sun comes up, and the night doesn't reset. Hari calls Amma and tells her the
   truth. Bittersweet: he misses his exam, the house still loses the beach, Advay leaves without apologising. But everyone is alive,
   and everyone has been seen.
2. **The Hollow Dawn.** Everyone lives, but Hari helped Raghavan. He has the referral letter. The village is blamed, the court stays
   the sealing, and the fence stays. The loop ends because nobody died, and that is the problem.
3. **The Witness.** Everyone lives and the fence opens by law, but Manikandan is fired and blamed, and Vastav never changes.
4. **The Drowned.** The nights run out. Hari wakes on the sand at dawn beside his own chappal, and Ponnamma paati closes his eyes.

**Disha, in each ending (only if Hari talked to her):**
- *Before Dawn:* she's at the rock, sketching the open beach. She sits beside Hari on the boat. It's the first morning she remembers
  him. She asks his name, and he tells her again.
- *Hollow Dawn:* she sees him standing with Raghavan as the village is blamed, and walks away.
- *The Witness:* she gives him her number; what happens next is left open.
- If he never talked to her, she isn't in any ending, and nobody tells the player what they missed.

**Disha's track, in full.** Nothing happens unless the player walks over to her. Every night she meets Hari for the first time, so the
romance is about learning her: what she's drawing, why architecture, her grandfather's house in Madurai, the song she hums. Get it
wrong (fake interest, quoting her own words back too obviously) and she pulls away for that night. Time with her is time not spent
saving someone. Talking to her makes her stay past 1 AM, which puts her at risk: in a loop where Hari doesn't stop it, she takes
Advay's ride at 5:30 and is in the SUV at the bend. Around the third loop she notices his damp shirt and asks "are you okay?", and
means it. Her 2 AM terrace scene, where she shows him her sketchbook, is one of the game's cutscenes.

## 12. How it looks and sounds
**Look (Shriram's direction):** a 3D, Telltale / *Life is Strange*-style narrative game. Today's slice is 2.5D (painted characters
in a 3D deck); the full game moves to 3D characters. Warm villa light against the indigo night, the orange sodium glow of ECR, the
cyan pool, the pink, washed-out dawn. The ordinary details (the checked shirt, the chappals, the cracked phone) carry the theme.
Not: glossy luxury ads, anime idols, a cartoon village, brand logos, or real people's likenesses.

**Sound:** two musics across one fence: the party's dance loop and the village's gaana (hand percussion, harmonium), crossfading as
you walk between them. The party music thins after 3 AM. At dawn both stop. The crash is heard, never seen; the true dawn is waves
and crows. On the road: the SUV's engine, the tea stall's radio, lorries passing. Every sound has a visual twin, so the game can be
played muted.

## 13. What's built today, and what it takes
**Built:** the A2 slice (loops 1–2 on the pool deck, 2.5D characters, the sound and music systems, the loop state and its tests)
and the A3 Blender props (the SUV, the rock shrine with the goddess, a fence segment), measured and checked in Godot.

**To build, in phases** (to be lined up with Assignments 4–10):
1. **Phase 1:** a 3D third-person Hari walking the villa and beach; 4–5 speaking characters; the ordinary guests.
2. **Phase 2:** the drivable SUV (Godot `VehicleBody3D`, on the A3 model) and the ECR stretch.
3. **Phase 3:** the kuppam; the second mystery; trust, notes, conclusions and colliding fixes; Disha's track.
4. **Phase 4:** cutscenes and the four endings.

The biggest single job is **rigged, animated 3D characters**. The scope stays a dense small world, not a GTA-style city.

**Engine:** Godot 4.7.2, GDScript, desktop (macOS). Blender for 3D. Every system keeps the headless-test habit from A1–A3.
