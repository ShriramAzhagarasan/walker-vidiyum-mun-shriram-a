Vidiyum Mun (விடியும் முன்) — "Before Dawn"
Full plot and design reference
Provenance key (for FRICTIONAL.md and SOURCES.md):
(Shriram) = decided by Shriram
(Claude) = proposed by Claude in chat; Shriram reviews and marks what he adopts

1. Logline
A middle-class Chennai boy goes to the last party on a rich family's private ECR beach and keeps waking up at 8 PM, reliving the same night, until he understands why dawn keeps going wrong, and that he is the one nobody noticed was missing. (premise: Shriram; "the one missing" twist: Claude)

2. Lore and facts
The village fishermen call the sea deity Kadal Amma. Her actual name is Manimekala Theivam. (Shriram)
Her shrine on the beach is just a rock, with no building. (Shriram)
In the Tamil epic Manimekalai, Manimekala is the sea goddess who rescues people from the sea and protects seafarers. This is why a shipping-logistics businessman would see her as the source of his luck, and why she is the reason for the loop. (Claude; verify before citing)
Before the private fence went up, the rock belonged to the village. Fishermen touched it and prayed before launching their boats at dawn. Since the fence, they pray to it from the other side of the wire. (Claude)

3. Setting
Place: a beach house on East Coast Road (ECR), Chennai. (Shriram)
The house is legal. The sand in front of it is not. That stretch of beach is public, but Vastav's father fenced it off and made it private because he is rich and capable of it. (Shriram)
Spaces: pool deck (above the beach, looking down at it), the private beach and the rock, the old private fence, the village side of the beach where the boats are, the driveway and gate, the house interior (later loops). The highway exists only as sound. (Shriram + Claude)
Time: one night, 8 PM to about 5:55 AM (dawn).

4. The core conflict
Why the beach was taken: Vastav's father believes that having the Manimekala rock to himself, and praying there alone, is why his shipping logistics business keeps winning. He is not trying to own a view. He is trying to own the goddess. (Shriram)
What happens tomorrow: At 7 AM, government officials arrive to seal off the beach area. A new fence will be built from the house down to the beach line. The beach, and the rock, become public again. (Shriram)
So tonight is the family's last night on "their" beach. Vastav throws the party on the sand itself, around the rock, with the village watching from the other side of the old fence. (Shriram)

5. Why the time loop exists (Claude)
Hari has already died once.
On the original night, before the game begins, a fire broke out on the village side at dawn. A 13-year-old village boy, Muthu, ran into the surf after a burning boat. Hari went in after him, pushed Muthu back toward shore, and was pulled under by a rip current.
Nobody at the party noticed he was gone. Nobody there knew his surname. Aadhav was already gone in the car. His mother thought he was at Aadhav's place studying. The only people who noticed were the village fishermen who pulled him out.
Manimekala Theivam rescues people from the sea. Vastav's father fenced her off believing she protects only him and his ships. On the night it mattered, she chose the outsider: the middle-class boy nobody at the party noticed. She cannot undo his drowning, so she gives him back the night, again and again, until it ends with no one lost.
The central irony: the rich man owns the goddess's rock, but she saves the boy he would never have noticed.
Loop rules
Every loop starts at 8 PM on the pool deck, with Amma's call.
Only Hari remembers. His phone notes carry across loops. This is the clue log.
Each loop, Hari wakes with his shirt slightly damp and sand in his pocket. Players notice this long before they understand it.
Dawn always resets the night, whatever happened, until dawn comes with no one lost, including Hari.
One person senses something: the old woman who prays at the rock from the village side of the fence.

6. Characters
Hari — protagonist (Shriram; details Claude)
22, final-year engineering student, middle-class Chennai family. Loosely based on Shriram.
Wants tonight: a referral into Vastav's father's shipping company. Placement season is going badly.
Risks: a 9:30 AM arrear exam the next morning, and the lie to Amma.
Look: ironed checked half-sleeve shirt, jeans, plain rubber chappals among everyone's sneakers, cracked phone screen, college ID still in his pocket.
Arc: from wanting to be let into this world, to seeing the people that world doesn't see.
Amma (call: Shriram; payoff: Claude)
Calls once, at 8 PM. That buzz starts every loop.
Hari tells her he is at Aadhav's place studying, and tells that same lie every loop.
Payoff: at the final dawn, Hari calls her back and tells her the truth.
Aadhav — Hari's friend (Shriram; details Claude)
Hari's school friend who drifted into the rich crowd through college. He brings Hari to the party.
Always beside Advay, because Advay is the rich, cool one and Aadhav wants into that world. He mirrors Hari, who wants the same thing through a referral.
Funny and warm in the early loops, which makes his fate hit harder.
Vastav — the host (Shriram; details Claude)
Charming, generous, the son of the house.
Wants one last good night on the beach before the officials arrive.
Hidden: as a small child, before the fence, he played on this beach with the village kids, including Selvam. He has watched his father pray alone at a rock that belongs to everyone, and part of him has always known it was wrong. (Claude)
Advay — Vastav's cousin (name: Shriram; details Claude)
26, recently back from studying abroad, loud, carries a Bluetooth speaker, calls everyone "bro."
Wants: to be the big man at the party.
His flaw: ego. After the village boys make him look small at midnight, he cannot let it go.
He is not a villain. He is a drunk, proud man whose small cruelty starts a chain he cannot control.
Manikandan — the driver (Shriram; backstory Claude)
Mid-50s, white uniform shirt with the sleeves rolled up, thin towel on his shoulder, leaning on the car, half a beedi.
Secret: he is from the village. Selvam is his nephew. Every day he drives the man who fenced off his own people's goddess.
Why he trusts Hari: in loop 1, Hari overhears him on the phone with Selvam. In loop 2, Hari asks after Selvam by name. Nobody at this party has ever asked Manikandan about his own life.
Selvam and the village boys (Shriram: "local guys who look like goons"; arc Claude)
Mid-20s fishermen drinking on the village side of the fence.
Arc: they look like trouble at midnight → they are witnesses who saw everything → they are the ones who pulled Hari out on the original night.
Muthu (Claude)
Selvam's 13-year-old brother. The boy Hari saved on the original night.
The old woman at the rock (Claude)
Prays at the rock from the village side of the fence every dawn. The only person who senses Hari is reliving the night. Name to be decided.
Perumal — the watchman (Claude)
Old man at the gate with a small radio. Sees who comes and goes. Dozes off around 2:30 AM.
Vastav's father (Shriram; absence Claude)
Never seen. Abroad, avoiding the sealing. Present only through his rock, his fence, and his phone calls to Vastav.

7. The night, if Hari does nothing (Claude, built on Shriram's 1:40 and dawn beats)
Time	What happens
8:00 PM	Amma's call. Hari on the pool deck. Loop start.
9:00 PM	The party moves down to the beach. Vastav's toast beside the rock. Hari asks about a referral and is politely brushed off.
10:30 PM	Vastav and Advay argue privately. Advay wants to "show the village boys whose beach it is." Vastav wants a quiet last night: officials come at 7, the family can't afford trouble. Advay: "Your dad bought this whole beach and you're scared of fishermen?"
Midnight	Advay starts throwing bottles over the fence. The village boys shout back. Selvam makes Advay look small in front of everyone.
1:40 AM	Advay takes the car keys from Manikandan and sends him to sleep: "I'll drive myself in the morning."
2:30 AM	Perumal dozes off at the gate. The party thins.
4:00 AM	Village fishermen start preparing their boats on their side of the fence.
4:45 AM	Still angry, Advay fires rockets toward the village side "for fun." One lands on drying nets beside a boat. The fire. Aadhav, standing beside Advay, tries to beat it out and badly burns his arm.
5:15 AM	Muthu runs into the surf after a burning boat. The village boys come over the fence looking for whoever fired the rockets. Someone has called the police.
5:30 AM	Advay grabs Aadhav, saying he's taking him to the hospital. Part of it is real, part of it is running away. Drunk, at dawn, on ECR. The crash.
~5:55 AM	Dawn. White-out. Loop.
One chain, not three tragedies: the fence creates the anger → the anger becomes the bottles → the humiliation becomes the rockets → the rockets become the fire → the fire becomes the drowning and the crash.

8. The story, loop by loop
Loop 1 — The night as it happens
Hari is a guest. He drifts, watches, makes small talk, asks for the referral. Near 1:40 he passes Manikandan on the phone with Selvam. At dawn: a horn, a brake screech from the highway, white-out. Learns: Advay took the keys at 1:40. Something happened on the road.
Loop 2 — The keys (the assignment slice)
Amma's call. Hari is back on the pool deck, shirt damp. He reaches Manikandan before 1:40 and asks after Selvam. Manikandan keeps the keys; Advay can't drive. At dawn, no crash, but the fire still happens and Aadhav's arm is still burned. End card: "…but someone is missing." (Muthu, though the player doesn't know yet.)
Loop 3 — The beach
Hari goes down to the sand and the fence. He meets the old woman at the rock and hears from Manikandan how the beach was taken. At 4:45 he sees Advay fire the rocket. Learns: the fire is Advay's. Muthu goes into the water.
Loop 4 — The sea
Hari tries to stop the fire and fails, then goes into the surf after Muthu. He gets Muthu back to shore and is pulled under. The player experiences what Hari doesn't yet understand. White-out. Learns: saving Muthu by himself costs Hari his life. The loop doesn't end.
Loop 5 — The chappal
Hari stays out of the water and gets Selvam to the surf in time. Muthu lives. At dawn, Hari finds his own rubber chappal at the tide line beside the rock, from a night he never lived in this loop. The old woman looks at him: he is "that boy." Learns: he already died once. The night is being given back to him.
Loop 6 — The root
Hari works backwards. The fire starts with the rocket, the rocket with midnight, and midnight with the fence. From Manikandan, he learns Vastav and Selvam played together on this beach as kids. Learns: the only real fix is for the fence to open, and only Vastav can open it.
Final loop — Before dawn
Hari doesn't ask for the referral. He brings Vastav to the fence before midnight. Vastav and Selvam talk for the first time in years. When Advay starts in with the bottles, Hari stands up to him in front of everyone, and this time Vastav backs Hari, not his cousin. (the mass moment) Before 4 AM, Vastav unlocks the old fence himself. The village prepares its boats on its own sand. No rocket, no fire, no crash.

9. The ending
The old woman touches the Manimekala rock for the first time in years, with Vastav standing beside her. The officials' cars arrive to find the fence already open. Hari sits on an overturned boat while Manikandan hands him tea from a flask. Aadhav, unhurt, is asleep on the sand. The sun comes up, and the night doesn't reset.
Hari calls Amma back and tells her the truth.
Cut to black.
Bittersweet, not perfect: Hari misses his exam. The house still loses the beach. Advay leaves without apologising. His friendship with Vastav is changed for good. But everyone is alive, and everyone has been seen.

10. Theme and tone
Theme in one word: seen. Hari learns that being invisible isn't the same as being unimportant.
Draft pillars: Every loop, you see more · The night belongs to Chennai · Dawn is always coming · The people they ignore know everything. (Claude, not yet approved)
Tone: grounded and specific, taken from Tamil films with this kind of plot. (Shriram) Comedy beats from Aadhav in early loops; one mass moment in the final loop; no song sequence.

11. Sound direction
Two kinds of music across the fence: the party's electronic dance music on the beach, and gaana-style street music (hand percussion, raw voices, harmonium) from the village side, both audible together.
Music as a clock: the party music thins as dawn nears; the village music stays. At dawn, both stop.
Never show the crash. Only sound: horn, brake screech, white-out.
The final dawn: waves and crows only.
All music generated from descriptions in words. No existing songs or recordings as prompts or references.

12. Art direction notes for free-model generation
Small cast, strong silhouettes. Each character gets one signature colour and shape: Hari's checked shirt and chappals, Advay's speaker, Manikandan's white shirt and towel, Selvam's lungi.
Night helps consistency. Strong single light sources (pool cyan, fire orange, dawn pink) hide small drift in faces and clothing.
Few locations: pool deck, beach and rock, fence, village boats, gate.
Portraits for emotion: dialogue portraits with expression rows are easier to keep consistent than subtle full-body animation.
No real people's likenesses, no film references, no brands in prompts. Describe the look in words.

13. The assignment slice
Playable space: the pool deck only, staged to look down onto the beach, the rock and the fence, so the conflict is visible from the first frame.
Loop 1: Advay takes the keys at 1:40; at dawn, horn, brake screech, white-out.
Loop 2: Amma's call wakes Hari; if he reaches Manikandan before 1:40, the keys stay.
End card: "…but someone is missing."
