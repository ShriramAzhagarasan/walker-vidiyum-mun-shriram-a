## My hand edit in Blender (2026-10-15, ~20:31–20:37, during the screen recording)
*Shriram's words. Claude drafted this paragraph from what Shriram had said and done in the session; Shriram read it and sent it back as their own ("ok").*

I duplicated the red sign twice, then realised that broke the course's 6-shape limit, so I deleted the copies. I moved the one sign to the centre of the fence and in front of the ropes. At first it looked like it was behind the ropes, and I learned that "front" and "back" depend on which side of the Y axis you're looking from. Dragging it by eye left it floating about 5 cm in front of the rope, which I couldn't see from the front. Typing exact values into the Item tab (X 0, Y −0.03) put it flush on the rope. What it taught me: how X, Y and Z work in Blender (X is along the fence, Y is in front of or behind it, Z is up), and that exact numbers in the sidebar are more reliable than dragging when something has to sit exactly on another part.

**What was measured along the way (read-only `get_scene_info` / Python by Claude):** after the duplicates the fence had 8 mesh objects (Claude pointed out the 6-shape limit); after the first move the sign sat at (−0.002, −0.085, 1.055), so the fence measured 0.16 m deep instead of 0.12; after Shriram typed the values it sat at (0.000, −0.030, 1.055), flush with the middle rope, and the file was saved. The edit is in `blender/fence_segment.blend` and in the exported `godot/assets/models/fence_segment.glb`.
